Federated Self-training for Semi-supervised Audio Recognition

Turn unlabeled audio into shared learning

Vasileios Tsouvalas · Aaqib Saeed · Tanir Ozcelebi

At a glance

Most device audio has no labels. FedSTAR turns selected local predictions into training targets, combining them with known labels before sharing model updates. Optional public-audio pretraining supplies a stronger starting point.

What this adds

  • 01

    Local self-training brings unlabeled audio into the same update as labeled examples.

  • 02

  • 03

Abstract

Federated Learning is a distributed machine learning paradigm dealing with decentralized and personal datasets. Since data reside on devices such as smartphones and virtual assistants, labeling is entrusted to the clients or labels are extracted in an automated way. Specifically, in the case of audio data, acquiring semantic annotations can be prohibitively expensive and time-consuming. As a result, an abundance of audio data remains unlabeled and unexploited on users’ devices. Most existing federated learning approaches focus on supervised learning without harnessing the unlabeled data. In this work, we study the problem of semi-supervised learning of audio models via self-training in conjunction with federated learning. We propose FedSTAR to exploit large-scale on-device unlabeled data to improve the generalization of audio recognition models. We further demonstrate that self-supervised pre-trained models can accelerate the training of on-device models, significantly improving convergence within fewer training rounds. We conduct experiments on diverse public audio classification datasets and investigate the performance of our models under varying percentages of labeled and unlabeled data. Notably, we show that with as little as 3% labeled data available, FedSTAR on average can improve the recognition rate by 13.28% compared to the fully supervised federated model.

How it works

FedSTAR

01

Joint local objective

The client learns from known labels and retained pseudo-labels in one update. β = 0.5 weights the unlabeled term in the reported experiments.

ℒk=ℒs+βℒu

ACM TECS §4.1–4.3, Eqs. 3 and 7. Both losses are computed before backpropagation.

02

Confidence-filtered pseudo-labels

Apply softmax to temperature-scaled logits, take the most likely class and retain predictions above τ. Temperature is 4; the cosine threshold increases from 0.5 to 0.9.

y^=argmaxcezc/T∑jezj/T

Eq. 5 and Table 2. Confidence filtering reduces exposure to uncertain targets; it does not guarantee correct labels.

03

Ordinary federated aggregation

A sampled set of clients trains locally and returns model parameters. FedAvg forms a data-weighted average; the self-training change is inside the client update.

Algorithm 1, PDF p. 12. Experiments simulate clients with the Flower framework.

04

Optional public-data initialization

Contrastive pretraining on FSD-50K supplies initial encoder weights. The projection layer is discarded before downstream learning.

§4.4 and Figure 6. Better performance after 10 rounds is distinct from final accuracy after 100 rounds.

Takeaways

  • Unlabeled audio helps under scarce labels: all three selected 15-client, 3%-label comparisons improve, by different amounts.
  • Data coverage matters: the class-skew experiment leaves randomly distributed unlabeled clips available across classes.
  • Initialization and final accuracy differ: faster early convergence does not ensure a better 100-round endpoint.
Study scope & sources
  • Evidence is from the 2022 ACM TECS journal article; the related ICASSP paper remains a separate publication.
  • Client training is simulated. Keeping raw audio local establishes data locality, not differential privacy.
Data
Ambient Acoustic Contexts (10 events), Speech Commands v2 (12 classes), VoxForge (6 languages); FSD-50K for optional contrastive pretraining.
Models
Four-block convolutional audio network using log-mel inputs, group normalization and spatial dropout.
Compared with
Centralized supervised training and supervised FedAvg with matching local labeled subsets; random versus pretrained FedSTAR.
Protocol
Flower simulations with 5–30 clients, 20–80% participation, 1–4 local epochs and three full runs per setting.

Scope & limitations

  • Self-training needs some reliable labels and assumes local labeled and unlabeled audio come from the same distribution.
  • The strongest class-skew gains use unlabeled data that may contain all classes even where labeled data do not.
  • More local training can harm the 3% label setting through overfitting or incorrect pseudo-labels; benefits are not universal across configurations.
  • Experiments simulate federated clients. Real-device energy, system heterogeneity and formal differential privacy are not established.
